## Swiss Employment Law Fundamentals
### Employment Contract Requirements
- Written contract recommended (oral valid but risky)
- Trial period: maximum 3 months
- Notice periods: 1 month (year 1), 2 months (years 2-9), 3 months (10+ years)
- Can be extended by contract but not reduced below legal minimum
- Collective bargaining agreements may impose sector requirements

### Working Time
- Standard: 40-42 hours/week in Geneva (sector dependent)
- Maximum: 45 hours/week (office), 50 hours/week (industry)
- Overtime: must be compensated (125% pay or time off)
- Recording: mandatory time tracking for all employees
- Sunday work: prohibited except with special authorization
- Night work: special protections and compensation

### Vacation and Leave
- Minimum vacation: 4 weeks per year (5 weeks if under 20)
- Public holidays: 11-13 days in Geneva (varies by employer)
- Sick leave: salary continuation per scale (100% for 3 weeks, decreasing)
- Maternity leave: 14 weeks at 80% salary (98 days)
- Paternity leave: 2 weeks at 80% salary
- Military service: salary continuation mandatory
- Vacation must be taken (cannot be paid out except at termination)

### Salary and Compensation
- No statutory minimum wage in Geneva (but collective agreements may apply)
- 13th month salary: not mandatory but very common practice
- Salary payment: monthly, typically end of month
- Payslip requirements: detailed breakdown mandatory
- Equal pay: Geneva requires analysis for employers with 100+ employees

## Social Insurance and Benefits
### Mandatory Employer Contributions (% of gross salary)
- AVS/AI/APG (Old age, disability): ~5.3% employer + 5.3% employee
- AC (Unemployment): ~1.1% employer + 1.1% employee
- LPP (Pension, 2nd pillar): age-dependent, ~7-18% total
- LAA (Accident insurance): ~1-3% employer (occupational only)
- LAAC (Non-occupational): if >8 hours/week, ~1-2% employee
- IJM Geneva (Sickness): ~1.4% employer + 0.5% employee
- Family allowances: ~2.5% employer

**Total employer burden: typically 20-30% on top of gross salary**

### LPP (Occupational Pension) Details
- Mandatory from CHF 22,050 annual salary
- Coordinated salary: gross - CHF 25,725 (2024)
- Age-based contribution rates:
  - 25-34: 7%
  - 35-44: 10%
  - 45-54: 15%
  - 55-65: 18%
- Split between employer/employee (at least 50% employer)
- Choose pension foundation carefully (fees, performance)

## Recruitment in Switzerland
### Hiring Process
1. Job posting (Swiss job boards, LinkedIn, agencies)
2. CV screening (Swiss CV format expectations)
3. Interviews (typically 2-3 rounds)
4. Reference checks (always conducted)
5. Employment contract negotiation
6. Work permit application (if non-Swiss/EU)
7. Background checks (criminal record extract if relevant)
8. Medical exam (if required by insurance)

### Work Permits for Foreign Nationals
#### EU/EFTA Citizens
- Easier process under bilateral agreements
- Short-term (<90 days): notification only
- Long-term: residence permit (L or B) required
- Family members can join and work

#### Non-EU/EFTA Citizens
- Limited quotas (difficult to obtain)
- Employer must prove no Swiss/EU candidate available
- Priority to highly qualified specialists
- Salary must meet Swiss standards
- Process: 2-3 months minimum
- Types: B permit (5 years), L permit (1 year)

## Performance Management
### Swiss Approach
- Less aggressive than US culture
- Emphasis on consensus and collaboration
- Direct feedback less common (more diplomatic)
- Annual performance reviews standard
- 360-degree feedback increasingly used
- PIPs (Performance Improvement Plans) must be fair and documented

### Performance Issues
1. Informal coaching and support
2. Written warning (first level)
3. Formal warning (second level)
4. Performance improvement plan with timeline
5. Termination (if no improvement and proper process followed)

*Swiss courts favor employees - documentation essential*

## Termination Procedures
### Termination with Notice
- Must respect legal/contractual notice periods
- Notice must be in writing (registered mail recommended)
- Cannot give notice during protected periods:
  - Sickness/accident: 30-180 days depending on tenure
  - Pregnancy and 16 weeks post-birth
  - Military service
  - Humanitarian missions (Red Cross, etc.)
- Garden leave possible but salary continues
- Vacation must be taken during notice period

### Termination without Notice (Exceptional)
- Only for serious cause (gross misconduct)
- Immediate dismissal
- High burden of proof
- Examples: theft, serious insubordination, violence
- Timing critical: must act immediately upon knowledge

### Termination Payments
- Salary through notice period
- Accrued vacation (if not taken)
- Pro-rata 13th month salary
- No severance required by law (unless contractual)
- Certificate of employment (certificat de travail) mandatory
- Social insurance notification

### Abusive Dismissal Protection
- Discriminatory reasons: protected characteristics
- Retaliation: whistleblowing, complaints
- During protected periods: see above
- Remedies: reinstatement (rare) or compensation (max 6 months salary)

## Employee Relations
### Common Issues
- Salary increases: typically annual, 0-5% based on performance/inflation
- Expense reimbursement: clear policy needed (home office, transport)

- Side activities: allowed if no conflict of interest, must declare

### Collective Bargaining
- Sector-specific agreements (construction, hospitality, etc.)
- May impose minimum wages, working conditions
- Binding if company is member of employer association
- Extension possible to entire sector by canton
